## ✅ Question 1: MCQ Answers & Explanations

1. **Answer: b) $p \to r$**
   - **Explanation:** මෙය ලොජික් වල "Hypothetical Syllogism" නියමයයි. "p නම් q වේ" සහ "q නම් r වේ" යන්නෙන් "p නම් r වේ" යන්න අදහස් වේ.
2. **Answer: a) $\exists x \forall y (P(x, y) \wedge \neg Q(x,y))$**
   - **Explanation:** $\neg(\forall x \exists y (P \to Q))$ යනු De Morgan's නීති අනුව, $\exists x \forall y \neg(P \to Q)$ බවට පත්වේ. $P \to Q$ හි ප්‍රතිෂේධය (Negation) වන්නේ $P \wedge \neg Q$ ය. 
   - **Common Mistake:** බොහෝ ළමුන් $P \to Q$ හි ප්‍රතිෂේධය $\neg P \to \neg Q$ යැයි සිතති. එය වැරදියි!
3. **Answer: c) 15**
   - **Explanation:** Euclidean algorithm: 240 = 105(2) + 30. 105 = 30(3) + 15. 30 = 15(2) + 0. අවසාන ශුන්‍ය නොවන ඉතිරිය 15 යි.
4. **Answer: c) 16**
   - **Explanation:** $A$ හි මූලද්‍රව්‍ය 5ක් ඇත. ඉන් 1 අනිවාර්යයෙන්ම තිබිය යුතු නම්, ඉතිරි මූලද්‍රව්‍ය 4 (2, 3, 4, 5) මඟින් සෑදිය හැකි උපකුලක ගණන $2^4 = 16$ කි.
5. **Answer: d) $p \to q, q ; \therefore p$**
   - **Explanation:** මෙය "Fallacy of Affirming the Consequent" ලෙස හැඳින්වේ. (උදා: වහිනවා නම් පාර තෙමෙනවා. පාර තෙමිලා, ඒ නිසා වහිනවා. මෙය වැරදියි, පාර වෙනත් හේතුවකටත් තෙමෙන්න පුළුවන්).
6. **Answer: None of the above (Let's say a specific one in a real paper, but conceptually checking mod)**
   - *(Mock note: For exam, $45 \equiv 11 \pmod{17}$. If an option evaluates to $11 \pmod{17}$, that is the answer).*
7. **Answer: b) 3**
   - **Explanation:** $14x \equiv 12 \pmod{30} \implies 7x \equiv 6 \pmod{15}$. $7 \times 3 = 21 \equiv 6 \pmod{15}$. x = 3.
8. **Answer: a) $(-1.5, 3)$**
   - **Explanation:** $-9 < 4x - 3 < 9 \implies -6 < 4x < 12 \implies -1.5 < x < 3$.
9. **Answer: c) Between any two distinct rational numbers, there is an irrational number.**
   - **Explanation:** මෙය තාත්වික සංඛ්‍යා පද්ධතියේ "Density property" එකයි. (a) වැරදියි, rational + irrational = අනිවාර්යයෙන්ම irrational වේ.

## ✅ Question 3: Structured Question Answers

### Part 1: Number Theory
**(a) Euclidean Algorithm:**
240 = 105(2) + **30**  `[+1 Mark]`
105 = **30**(3) + 15   `[+1 Mark]`
30 = 15(**2**) + 0     `[+1 Mark]`

**(b) GCD:**
$\gcd(240, 105) = 15$ `[+1 Mark]`

**(c) Linear Combination:**
15 = 105 - 3(**30**) `[+1 Mark]`
= 105 - 3(240 - 2(**105**)) `[+1 Mark]`
= **7**(105) - 3(240) `[+1 Mark]`
*(පැහැදිලි කිරීම: -3 x -2 = +6 යි. කලිං තිබුණු 105 ත් එක්ක එකතු වුණාම 7 ක් වෙනවා! මේ සුළුකිරීම ගොඩක් අයට වරදින තැනක්).*

**(d) Multiplicative Inverse of 7 mod 26:**
26 = 7(3) + 5
7 = 5(1) + 2
5 = 2(2) + 1
1 = 5 - 2(2) = 5 - 2(7 - 5) = 3(5) - 2(7) = 3(26 - 7(3)) - 2(7) = 3(26) - 11(7).
Since $-11(7) \equiv 1 \pmod{26}$, the inverse is $-11 \equiv 15 \pmod{26}$.

### (c) Proof by Contradiction
**Statement:** If $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even.
**Proof:**
Assume for the sake of contradiction that $n^2$ is even, BUT $n$ is NOT even (i.e., $n$ is odd). `[+1 Mark]`
If $n$ is odd, then by definition, $n = 2k + 1$ for some integer $k$. `[+1 Mark]`
Now, let's look at $n^2$:
$n^2 = (2k + 1)^2$
$n^2 = 4k^2 + 4k + 1$ `[+1 Mark]`
$n^2 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$
Let $m = 2k^2 + 2k$, which is an integer.
Then $n^2 = 2m + 1$. `[+1 Mark]`
This implies that $n^2$ is odd.
But this contradicts our initial premise that $n^2$ is even! `[+1 Mark]`
Therefore, our assumption that $n$ is odd must be false.
Hence, $n$ must be even. $\blacksquare$ `[+1 Mark]`

### (d) Mathematical Induction
**Prove:** $\sum_{k=1}^n (2k - 1) = n^2$

**Base Case ($n=1$):**
LHS = $2(1) - 1 = 1$.
RHS = $1^2 = 1$.
LHS = RHS. True for $n=1$. `[+1 Mark]`

**Inductive Hypothesis:**
Assume the statement is true for some arbitrary positive integer $p$.
That is, $\sum_{k=1}^p (2k - 1) = p^2$. `[+1 Mark]`

**Inductive Step:**
We must prove it is true for $n = p + 1$.
LHS for $n = p + 1$:
$\sum_{k=1}^{p+1} (2k - 1) = \sum_{k=1}^p (2k - 1) + [2(p + 1) - 1]$ `[+2 Marks]`
Substitute the hypothesis:
LHS = $p^2 + [2p + 2 - 1]$
LHS = $p^2 + 2p + 1$ `[+1 Mark]`
Notice that this is a perfect square!
LHS = $(p + 1)^2$.
This matches the RHS for $n = p + 1$. `[+1 Mark]`

**Conclusion:**
By the principle of mathematical induction, the statement is true for all positive integers $n$. `[+1 Mark]`

### (e) Set Theory Proof
**Prove:** $A \cap (B \setminus C) = (A \cap B) \setminus C$.

Let $x \in A \cap (B \setminus C)$.
$\iff x \in A \wedge x \in (B \setminus C)$ (By definition of intersection) `[+1 Mark]`
$\iff x \in A \wedge (x \in B \wedge x \notin C)$ (By definition of difference) `[+1 Mark]`
$\iff (x \in A \wedge x \in B) \wedge x \notin C$ (By associativity of $\wedge$) `[+1 Mark]`
$\iff x \in (A \cap B) \wedge x \notin C$ (By definition of intersection) `[+1 Mark]`
$\iff x \in (A \cap B) \setminus C$ (By definition of difference) `[+1 Mark]`
Therefore, $A \cap (B \setminus C) = (A \cap B) \setminus C$. $\blacksquare$
